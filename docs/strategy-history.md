# Strategy history and experiment decisions

The original task began with a pass-only JavaScript starter. The first complete action export contains 480 pass actions from the team. A move-east example was explained as a tutorial; it is not an evidenced competition strategy.

V1 implemented terrain-cost routing over known cells, persistent map memory, gold collection, deposits, a three-worker opening costing 5+6 iron, and a return/deposit deadline. The first toy experiment compared one/two/three workers under a trip-rate heuristic: means 114.67/197.92/277.75 gold. With three workers, nearest-gold selection and less eager partial returns averaged 307.33, beating the rate variant on all 12 paired seeds. Target selection and partial-return behavior changed together, so their separate contributions were not isolated.

V2 fixed a reproduced allocation bug: an incoming worker could reserve all remaining ore and make an on-site miner idle. On-site miner priority raised the nearest-gold toy mean to 321.25 across the same 12 seeds (10 wins, 1 tie, 1 loss). The V2 official log later recorded 199 gold banked by turn 478. Toy and official scores are not directly comparable.

V3 added a possible second base, supported by iron gathering, with a conservative projected-travel-savings criterion. On 12 solo 30x30 maps with iron, its mean was 268.33 versus V2's 234.58; it built on eight maps and skipped four. In the supplied official game it scored 193 and lost a gold tie on iron, 4 versus 6. It did not attempt a base in that game. Only states 0–189 were present in the shared telemetry, even though the action export was complete.

V4 tested global greedy ore assignment, a small target-continuity preference, and exploration footprints. Initial means: V3 baseline 268.33; ore assignment 281.50; exploration spreading 272.25; both 279.92. The combined exploratory run included one invalid action. A later construction guard accounted for teammate movement onto a build site. Ore assignment fell to 262.00 versus V3's 264.83 on fresh seeds. Across 24 paired maps the mean difference was +5.17 with standard error 8.43: too uncertain to establish a reliable improvement. Exploration spreading remains disabled. Saved snapshots include later guard/logging fixes; old experiment byte-for-byte source was not captured.

V5 added observed-depletion/claim responses, projected depletion from visible enemy miners, target-change hysteresis, project cancellation and revisiting old ground. Eight focused reaction scenarios passed. Its solo mean across the 24 familiar seeds was 270.58 versus V4's 271.75, so those tests did not demonstrate a score gain. The latest supplied official practice game was a win: 204 banked gold, runner-up 188. The log confirms 204 gold extracted from 207 mine attempts, zero failed moves and empty final logged inventories.

V6 focuses on an identified planning weakness: the old base planner charged a fixed 24-turn iron-trip allowance. It now prices known funding routes and missing iron, models deposit/construction steps, returns iron from cancelled projects, and sends a funded builder's existing cargo home before construction. Uniform solo means over 24 paired seeds: 278.96 versus V5's 270.58. Rich-center solo means over 24 paired seeds: 384.96 versus 381.50. Both differences are small relative to variation; official play has not yet verified an improvement. Center-directed exploration averaged 349.00 versus 369.67 for route pricing alone on its initial rich-center seeds, so the shipped option is disabled.

The Garrett review concerned separate teammate code and 16-team action data. It motivates early productive hubs, fuller loads and better late regional reassignment. Its 336 mining attempts are not a verified gold score; exact locations, source and final standings were not supplied.

See data/experiment-index.json for the original result paths, configured variants, simulator families and overlap between datasets. Do not count each JSON container as a separate experiment: several files repeat the same rows for packaging.
